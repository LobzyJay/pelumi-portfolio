Trafficking networks now run on encrypted platforms, online recruitment and cryptocurrency, while the U.S. response stays split across law enforcement, financial regulators and intelligence agencies that rarely share a common picture. This memo, addressed to the Director of National Intelligence, argues that cyber-enabled trafficking is not consistently treated as a national-security threat in its own right — so intelligence stays reactive, targeting individual actors instead of dismantling networks early. It recommends three moves: designate cyber-enabled trafficking a standing Intelligence Community priority; fuse cyber, financial and trafficking intelligence into joint teams drawing on NSA, CIA, Treasury and FBI expertise; and strengthen intelligence support for disrupting trafficking's financial and overseas infrastructure — the scam compounds and laundering networks that keep it profitable.

The U.S. lists 60 minerals as critical to its energy transition, semiconductor industry and defence systems, yet depends on imports for most of them, and China controls up to 90% of global processing. This paper, addressed to the DOE's Office of Critical Minerals and Materials, argues the core U.S. weakness isn't a lack of technology — AI-enabled exploration, autonomous mining and advanced recycling all already exist — but a lack of coordination in scaling it. It recommends five moves: fast-track permitting with real environmental safeguards; a DARPA-style innovation and commercialisation fund; targeted incentives to expand domestic processing, refining and recycling; stronger alliances with partners like Australia, Canada and Japan alongside U.S. participation in deep-sea mineral governance; and a national data system paired with workforce investment to sustain all of it.

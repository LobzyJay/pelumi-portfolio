The EU, the U.S. and China start from incompatible premises on privacy, human rights and autonomous weapons — GDPR-style data protection against China's more permissive state-access model; the EU's rights-centred AI Act against Beijing's emphasis on stability and control; Western caution on lethal autonomous weapons against active development elsewhere. This memo, addressed to the UN Secretary-General, argues that chasing one binding global framework across these divides is the wrong goal. Instead it proposes four pragmatic steps: multilateral dialogue around principles all sides can share — fairness, accountability, minimising harm; issue-specific working groups on data protection, transparency and autonomous weapons; cross-border governance sandboxes where new rules can be tested before they're scaled; and a non-binding international AI ethics charter as a foundation for later, more detailed regulation.

Sri Lanka is recovering from one of the most severe economic crises in its post-independence history. This 27-page National Development Plan, co-authored with **Manaal Shaikh**, sets a five-year strategy (2026–2031) built around three prosperity goals: economic and political stability, climate resilience, and social well-being. It opens with a lesson from why previous plans failed — weak leadership, poor inter-ministry coordination, and policy made without civil society or local communities at the table — and builds its baseline assessment, budget, timeline, monitoring framework and delegation guidance around not repeating it. The audience is deliberately wide: government ministries, international development partners, civil-society organisations, private-sector investors and the communities the plan is meant to serve.
